All important formulas for CBSE Class 10 Science — Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Perfect for last-minute revision.

Chapter 9 · Light — Reflection and Refraction
Mirror FormulaCh 9
1/v + 1/u = 1/f

u = object distance, v = image distance, f = focal length (sign convention applies)

Lens FormulaCh 9
1/v − 1/u = 1/f

For convex and concave lenses with the Cartesian sign convention.

Magnification (Mirror)Ch 9
m = −v/u = h′/h

h′ = image height, h = object height. Negative m ⇒ real & inverted image.

Magnification (Lens)Ch 9
m = v/u = h′/h

Positive m ⇒ virtual & erect image.

Snell's Law (Refractive Index)Ch 9
n = sin i / sin r  ·  n = c / v

i = angle of incidence, r = angle of refraction, c = speed of light in vacuum, v = speed in medium.

Power of a LensCh 9
P = 1/f (f in metres)

Unit: dioptre (D). Convex lens ⇒ +P, Concave lens ⇒ −P. Combination: P = P₁ + P₂ + …

Chapter 10 · Human Eye and the Colourful World
Power of Correction LensCh 10
P = 1/f

Myopia ⇒ concave lens (−ve). Hypermetropia ⇒ convex lens (+ve). Presbyopia ⇒ bifocal lens.

Chapter 11 · Electricity
Ohm's LawCh 11
V = I R

V = potential difference (volt), I = current (ampere), R = resistance (ohm).

ResistivityCh 11
R = ρL / A

ρ = resistivity, L = length of conductor, A = area of cross-section.

Resistors in SeriesCh 11
R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃

Same current through each resistor; total resistance increases.

Resistors in ParallelCh 11
1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃

Same potential difference across each resistor; total resistance decreases.

Electric PowerCh 11
P = V I = I²R = V²/R

Unit: watt (W). Commercial energy: E = P × t (kWh, 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J).

Joule's Law of HeatingCh 11
H = I² R t

Heat produced in a resistor. Used in electric heater, fuse, bulb filament.

Electric ChargeCh 11
Q = I t

Unit: coulomb (C). Work done: W = V Q.

Chapter 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
Force on a Current-Carrying ConductorCh 12
F = B I L sin θ

Maximum when conductor is perpendicular to field (θ = 90°). Direction: Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.

Key Rules (no formula, must-know)Ch 12
Right-hand thumb rule · Fleming's LHR & RHR

Thumb rule → field around a straight conductor. LHR → motor (force). RHR → generator (induced current).

Chapter 1 · Chemical Reactions and Equations
Burning of MagnesiumCh 1
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Combination reaction — dazzling white flame, white ash (magnesium oxide).

Quicklime + WaterCh 1
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + heat

Exothermic combination; slaked lime is used in whitewashing.

Thermal DecompositionCh 1
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

Limestone decomposes on heating. Also: 2FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃.

Photochemical DecompositionCh 1
2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂ (sunlight)

Also 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂. Used in black-and-white photography.

Displacement ReactionCh 1
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

More reactive zinc displaces copper; blue colour fades to green.

Double Displacement (Precipitation)Ch 1
Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl

White precipitate of barium sulphate.

Respiration (Exothermic)Ch 1
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy

Decomposition + exothermic — energy is released.

Chapter 2 · Acids, Bases and Salts
NeutralisationCh 2
Acid + Base → Salt + Water

Example: NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O.

Acid + Metal CarbonateCh 2
Metal carbonate + Acid → Salt + CO₂ + H₂O

CO₂ turns lime water milky: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O.

Chlor-alkali ProcessCh 2
2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂

Electrolysis of brine gives sodium hydroxide, chlorine and hydrogen.

pH Scale (concept)Ch 2
pH < 7 acid · pH = 7 neutral · pH > 7 base

Our body works at pH 7.0–7.8; blood ~7.4. Acid rain: pH below 5.6.

Chapter 3 · Metals and Non-metals
Thermite ReactionCh 3
Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + heat

Highly exothermic — used to join railway tracks.

Metal + Water/Acid (pattern)Ch 3
Metal + Acid → Salt + H₂↑

Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.

Extraction of MercuryCh 3
2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂ ; 2HgO → 2Hg + O₂

Cinnabar is roasted, then reduced by heating alone.

Chapter 4 · Carbon and its Compounds
Combustion of Carbon CompoundsCh 4
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + heat & light

Complete combustion. Saturated hydrocarbons burn with a clean flame.

EsterificationCh 4
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

Acid + alcohol → ester (sweet smell), in presence of conc. H₂SO₄.

General Formulas (Homologous Series)Ch 4
Alkane CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ · Alkene CₙH₂ₙ · Alkyne CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

Alcohol: −OH, Aldehyde: −CHO, Ketone: >C=O, Acid: −COOH.

Chapter 5 · Life Processes
PhotosynthesisCh 5
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

In the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll. Occurs in chloroplasts.

Aerobic RespirationCh 5
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (ATP)

Complete breakdown of glucose in mitochondria.

Anaerobic RespirationCh 5
Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ + energy (yeast)

In muscles: Glucose → Lactic acid + energy (causes cramps).

Energy Currency of the CellCh 5
ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)

Produced during respiration; used for all cellular work.

Chapter 6 · Control and Coordination
Reflex Arc PathwayCh 6
Receptor → Sensory neuron → Spinal cord → Motor neuron → Effector

Rapid, involuntary response; relay neuron present in the spinal cord.

Key Hormones (must-know)Ch 6
Insulin · Adrenaline · Thyroxin · Testosterone · Oestrogen

Insulin (pancreas) regulates blood sugar; adrenaline prepares body for emergency; thyroxin controls metabolism.

Chapter 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce?
Human Female Reproductive PathwayCh 7
Ovary → Oviduct (fallopian tube) → Uterus

Fertilisation occurs in the oviduct; implantation in the uterus.

Unisexual vs BisexualCh 7
Flower: stamen (♂) + carpel (♀)

Pollination → fertilisation → seed (embryo) + fruit (ovary). Double fertilisation in plants.

Chapter 8 · Heredity
Mendel's Monohybrid RatioCh 8
F₂ phenotypic ratio = 3 : 1

Genotypic ratio 1 : 2 : 1 (TT : Tt : tt). Dominant trait masks recessive.

Mendel's Dihybrid RatioCh 8
F₂ phenotypic ratio = 9 : 3 : 3 : 1

Shows independent assortment of two trait pairs.

Sex DeterminationCh 8
XX = female · XY = male

Father's chromosome decides the sex of the child; probability 50%.

Chapter 13 · Our Environment
10% Law of Energy TransferCh 13
Only ~10% energy passes to the next trophic level

Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary consumer → Tertiary consumer.

Ozone FormationCh 13
O₂ + O → O₃ (UV radiation)

Ozone shields Earth from UV; depleted by CFCs.
